## Findings

- All ten trips need an explicit day-shape contract. Single-outing days are often intentional, but supporting time is buried in attraction notes and cannot be reliably rendered or validated.
- Washington Days 3 and 5 are empty canonical transfer days. Olympic Day 4 explicitly depends on reopening rather than offering a currently usable day. These are substantive content gaps.
- Most exact clock times are illustrative despite missing arrival/flight/operator schedules. Big Bend already uses useful relative timing; sunrise and wildlife light justify a window, not an invented date-independent clock.
- Arrival, transfer and departure days are mixed with “full sightseeing” claims. Glacier Day 1 and Acadia Day 2 illustrate overview/detail inconsistency.
- Measured within-day edges cover only a small fraction of actual travel. Inter-base and base-to-outing travel are usually narrative only; no measured duration must be invented to fill this gap.

## Contract diagnosis

The model-first schema strips property descriptions, including the existing instruction to prefer timeWindow over exact startTime. It exposes stops and lodging but no non-place day structure. The guard checks total activity + measured transfer minutes against 24 hours, but not stop chronology, elapsed gaps, justified precision, empty transfer days or explicitly accounted dayparts. Lodging-base folding can delete the only representation of relocation logistics when another attraction is present. The renderer sees a list of stops, so meals, rest, arrivals and major transfers exist only if buried in notes. The fix belongs to the ordinary planner contract and shared renderer, not destination fixtures.

## Clarified planning principle

The follow-up request makes dayparts the presentation skeleton, with exact times as optional commitments. The model first chooses each day's role within the whole trip; examples for three/five days are guidance rather than mandatory arrival/departure templates. The implementation therefore declares the useful start/end dayparts and covers that span, rather than requiring four separate activities or inventing a morning before arrival. A single block can span morning through afternoon. Geographic feasibility of unmeasured base journeys remains model judgment and an explicit uncertainty; the guard can require that a changed base has a transfer block but cannot verify a journey without a sourced route.
